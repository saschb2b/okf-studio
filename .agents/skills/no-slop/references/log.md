## 2026-07-30

**Update.** Added two concepts from the Microsoft Writing Style Guide and linked both from the root index. [microsoft-style.md](microsoft-style.md) records the mechanics no-slop adopts (the expletive opener, verb-first statements, the hollow "you can", heading end punctuation, the serial comma, single spacing), the two rules where Microsoft independently reaches an existing no-slop position (one word for one thing, sentence-case headings), and the three places its brand voice contradicts the skill. Those three are the em dash, which Microsoft recommends unspaced and no-slop bans; contractions, which Microsoft requires to project friendliness; and warmth itself, from which the other two follow. The conflict is genuine rather than a misreading, since Microsoft writes consumer product UI for a global audience while no-slop targets a reserved professional register, so the page resolves it with a third mode. House-style mode inverts the three when the text ships into a surface that already follows the guide, and leaves every other tell in force. [inclusive-and-accessible.md](inclusive-and-accessible.md) covers two failures the rest of the skill could not detect, prose that breaks for a reader using assistive technology and language that excludes a reader by assuming who they are. Neither reads as slop, and volume production introduces both quickly. Three of its phrase-level rules were promoted into the SKILL.md tells table (expletive opener, empty link text, directional-only reference), and two formatting rules into [formatting.md](formatting.md). Sources are the guide's top-ten, brand-voice, word-choice, accessibility, and bias-free pages, cited rather than reproduced, plus the Linguistic Society of America inclusive-language guidelines that Microsoft itself points to. No live-web crawl beyond those pages. Validated with `okf-validate --strict`.
