* **Fix**: In the **native app only**, the whole chrome could **scroll out of the window** — wheel-scrolling past the reader's end shifted the app up and left phantom space below, and it couldn't always be scrolled back. Root cause: `html`/`body` had no overflow control, and under WebKitGTK's sub-pixel viewport rounding (fractional display scaling) the document ends up a hair taller than the viewport — a real scroll container. A pane's scroll then **chains** to the document, and WebKit's `scrollIntoView` (outline jumps, heading permalinks) scrolls it **programmatically** too. Fix: `overflow: clip` on the document root (with `hidden` as the older-engine fallback, plus `overscroll-behavior: none`) — clip removes the scroll container entirely, so neither user input nor programmatic scrolling can ever move the shell. Verified by simulation: with 3000px of forced body overflow, `scrollTop` writes and `scrollIntoView` both leave the document at 0 while the reader pane still scrolls internally. Recorded in [Theming](ux/theming.md) (native chrome).
